
These are proposals. None is source-admitted by this packet.

```text

```

# 2. Core research question

> **What, exactly, does the biblical and earliest recoverable Christian record treat as invariant when “the Name” crosses language, speaker, script, time, referent, authority, invocation, presence, and community?**

Subquestions:

1. What are the relevant Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek name forms and what transformations connect them?
2. Where does identical or near-identical surface form refer to different persons?
3. Where does the same referent appear under different linguistic/scribal surfaces?
4. What operations are actually predicated of “the Name”: naming, calling, believing, invoking, gathering, baptizing, acting, suffering, bearing, glorifying, asking, being saved, etc.?
5. When `name` language appears, does the source-local syntax point primarily to label, referent, authority, reputation, possession/belonging, invocation, presence, or another function?
6. Which later doctrinal readings are genuinely source-local and which are later reception layers?
7. Do bind/loose, gathering, agreement, Spirit/body, river/breath/voice, and Name motifs form attributable source-local continuities or only later analytical rhyme?

> What kind of identity continuity is claimed for Jesus across pre-earthly, earthly, resurrected, exalted, liturgical, manuscript, and later reception contexts?

# 15. Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. manuscript/facsimile witnesses where material form matters;
2. current critical texts/editions with apparatus where textual variation matters;
3. lexica/grammars for morphology and usage;
4. primary Jewish/Greco-Roman comparanda when source-local idiom requires them;
5. early Christian reception witnesses;
6. modern scholarship representing competing readings;
7. later devotional/theological reception as reception, not source-local proof.

For every web/database source preserve edition/version and stable locator.

# 16. Failure modes

The dig fails methodologically if it:

- begins with gematria/frequency and hunts only confirming verses;
- treats English homonyms as source-language relationships;
- treats Jesus/Joshua same-form evidence as automatically theological;
- backfills later Trinitarian or liturgical formulations into earlier manuscript cuts without labeling reception;
- mistakes nomina sacra expansion for visible manuscript letters;
- treats repeated biblical imagery as independent corroboration without dependency analysis;
- treats symbolic fit as historical transmission;
- cannot return “ordinary name/authority idiom is sufficient”;
- cannot return “multiple incompatible Name-functions remain.”
