## Finding

The proposed Minnesota branch does not need a novel “private church” theory to obtain a coherent legal shape. Minnesota Chapter 315 already supplies a religious-corporation framework that can encode denominational relationship, trusteeship, membership, property, and temporal governance. Federal §508(c)(1)(A) is not a separate exemption from §501(c)(3); it is a filing exception for churches, integrated auxiliaries, and conventions/associations of churches that actually satisfy the substantive §501(c)(3) requirements.

Of the six questions prepared for counsel, four can be narrowed substantially from primary law. Two remain materially counsel-dependent:

1. **§315.01(3) vs. §315.21** — answerable as a conditional design choice.
2. **Can a Nigerian parent hold the appointment/recognition role?** — strong statutory support, but no Minnesota authority located directly testing a foreign denominational appointing body; Nigerian legal identity/authority documents also remain unverified.
3. **What exact certificate is recorded?** — core statutory contents are answerable, but the procedural interaction between §315.01(3) and §315.03 should be confirmed with the county recorder/counsel.
4. **Which powers should remain locally fiduciary?** — strongly answerable from Chapter 315 plus federal foreign-grant control principles.
5. **Separate trust now or later?** — design recommendation strongly favors deferral until a defined asset/restriction exists; not a statutory requirement.

# Six-question analysis

## Q1 — §315.21 or §315.01(3)?

**Status: SUBSTANTIALLY ANSWERED, conditional on intended polity.**

### §315.01(3)

Minn. Stat. §315.01(3) applies when the **constitution, rules, or usages** of a church, denomination, congregation, or religious society require trustees to be chosen by a minister, presiding elder, other officers, or **a body**. The selector (or presiding officer and secretary of the selecting body) executes and acknowledges a certificate naming the trustees, the time and selecting person/body, and the corporate name. Once recorded, the trustees and successors become the religious corporation.

This is the cleaner statutory route if the actual intended polity is:

```text
Impact Makers Global
  -> governing constitution/rules/usages
  -> parent officer/body selects MN trustees
  -> trustees hold MN temporal corporate body
```

### §315.21

Minn. Stat. §315.21 is an alternative route for a church/religious society that **does not wish to form under §§315.01-.20**. At least three members execute a certificate stating:
- name, general purpose, plan of operation, location;
- terms of admission;
- membership qualifications;
- officer selection;
- vacancies;
- management.

It is the cleaner route if the intended polity is:

```text
MN members constitute local religious corporation
  -> certificate defines membership / management
  -> affiliation covenant connects MN to Nigeria
```

It is especially attractive for a PMA-like membership covenant because membership mechanics are express statutory certificate content. It has a formal amendment mechanism in §§315.31-.32.

### Present architecture call

For an actual **branch** whose trustees are meant to be appointed/recognized upstream, §315.01(3) currently fits the stated relationship more directly. Membership covenant terms can remain in subordinate governing instruments without converting the formation route into §315.21.

If the parties want Minnesota members ultimately to own trustee-selection power, with Nigeria holding only ecclesiastical affiliation/recognition, §315.21 becomes more attractive.

**Do not silently combine the two as co-equal formation routes.** §315.21 expressly begins from the choice not to form under §§315.01-.20.

## Q2 — Can the Nigerian denominational body hold the appointment / recognition role?

**Status: PARTIALLY ANSWERED; strongest remaining legal discriminator.**

### What the statute supports

§315.01(3) says trustees may be chosen by:
- a minister;
- presiding elder;
- other officers;
- **a body**,

when the constitution, rules, or usages of a church/denomination/congregation/religious society require that mode.

The text contains no express Minnesota-residency, U.S.-citizenship, domestic-incorporation, or in-state-location requirement for the **appointing person/body**.

By contrast, Chapter 315 provisions that intend residency requirements can state them expressly. For example, §315.16 contains residency language for certain diocesan incorporators. That contrast supports, but does not conclusively prove, the reading that §315.01(3)'s appointing “body” is not geographically limited.

IRS guidance also recognizes religious organizations operating **under the authority of a religious body that is a church or denomination**, which is consistent with hierarchical cross-entity ecclesiastical structures.

### What is not yet established

No Minnesota appellate case was located in this pass directly deciding that a **foreign Nigerian church body** qualifies as the appointing “body” for §315.01(3).

More importantly, the factual predicate is not yet in hand:
- What is the Nigerian legal entity's exact name?
- Does it have a constitution/rule/canon that actually **requires** or authorizes branch trustee appointment?
- Which officer/body possesses that power?
- Is the officer/body legally continuous and documentable?
- Does “Rock Impact Makers International Believers Ministry,” “Impact Makers International,” or another entity own the ecclesiastical identity?

The public website is insufficient to answer those.

### Working answer

```text
FOREIGN LOCATION
does not appear textually disqualifying

BUT

FOREIGN BODY'S IDENTITY + ACTUAL CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY
remain prerequisites
```

This should be presented to counsel as a confirmation question, not as an open-ended architecture question.

## Q3 — What exact certificate gets recorded?

**Status: CORE CONTENT ANSWERED; PROCEDURAL DETAIL PARTIALLY UNRESOLVED.**

### If §315.01(3)

The statute expressly requires the selector (or presiding officer + secretary of the selecting body) to execute, acknowledge, and deliver a certificate stating:

1. names of trustees;
2. time they were chosen;
3. person or body by which they were chosen;
4. corporate name.

The certificate must then be “recorded as required by law.”

§315.03 states that the certificate is recorded with the **county recorder of the county where the society's place of worship is located** and refers to an acknowledgment plus a notice-of-meeting/affidavit package.

### Procedural tension

The notice/affidavit language in §315.03 maps naturally to the **election meeting** in §315.01(2), but §315.01(3) is an appointment route and does not itself require that meeting/posted notice.

Therefore the safest current formulation is:

```text
§315.01(3) certificate contents = known
acknowledgment = known
county recorder / county of place of worship = strongly indicated
whether §315.03 notice + posting affidavit is demanded on appointment route
= recorder/counsel confirmation needed
```

### If §315.21

The certificate contents are less ambiguous:
- name;
- general purpose;
- plan of operation;
- location;
- admission terms;
- membership qualifications;
- selection of officers;
- vacancy rules;
- management.

It is recorded with the county recorder of the county of its location.

## Q4 — Which powers should remain locally fiduciary even if ecclesiastical authority resides upstream?

**Status: SUBSTANTIALLY ANSWERED.**

Chapter 315 itself distinguishes trustee temporal power from ecclesiastical life.

§315.04 gives trustees power over church temporalities, property, claims, buildings, and title.

§315.05 gives trustees management authority over temporal affairs and money, subject in several matters to congregation/society direction or approval.

§315.12 makes real-property disposition a particularly explicit local statutory act: trustee action, member authorization, notice, and compliance with recorded denominational rules/certificate.

Federal charitable-contribution doctrine adds a cross-border constraint: deductible U.S. donations to a domestic charity cannot simply be earmarked as pass-through funds to a foreign organization. The domestic charity must approve a foreign program as furthering its own exempt purposes and retain control over use of the funds.

### Proposed authority partition

**Nigeria / ecclesiastical upstream**
- doctrine / statement of faith;
- ecclesiastical recognition of branch;
- ministerial credentialing / spiritual office, as agreed;
- branch identity/name license;
- church discipline / denominational process;
- trustee appointment/removal if the chosen Chapter 315 route lawfully assigns that authority upstream.

**Minnesota trustees / local fiduciary-temporal body**
- local bank accounts and signers;
- leases and ordinary contracts;
- local employment/payroll compliance;
- insurance;
- bookkeeping and financial records;
- restricted-gift compliance;
- acceptance/holding/disposition of Minnesota property;
- statutory member approvals where required;
- local tax/registration compliance;
- local donor acknowledgments;
- approval, restriction, monitoring, and documentation of any grant/support sent to Nigeria;
- local liabilities and litigation response.

### Critical non-collapse

```text
NIGERIA MAY APPOINT A TRUSTEE
!=
NIGERIA MAY WITHDRAW MN FUNDS AT WILL

ECCLESIASTICAL SUPERVISION
!=
DOMESTIC CHARITY AS FOREIGN CONDUIT

TRUSTEE
!=
BENEFICIAL OWNER
```

This is a strong Corpus OS fit: every exercise of authority should carry source, role, scope, concurrence, and affected property/funds.

## Q5 — Should property remain in the Chapter 315 body initially, with a separate trust deferred?

**Status: STRONG DESIGN RECOMMENDATION; not a legal requirement.**

Chapter 315 already allows a religious corporation to own real/personal property (§315.09), and separately recognizes property held by trustees in trust for a religious society (§315.14).

Minnesota §501B.36 also recognizes a separate charitable trust organized and operated exclusively for religious purposes and administered by a Chapter 315 or 317A religious association, exempting it from the specified charitable-trust registration/reporting provisions.

Nothing found requires a newly formed church to create a separate trust before it has:
- real estate;
- endowment assets;
- a donor-restricted corpus;
- denominationally restricted property;
- a concrete asset-isolation reason.

### Present recommendation

Start with:

```text
MN Chapter 315 church
  -> operating funds
  -> ordinary equipment
  -> leases/contracts
  -> initial ministry assets
```

Defer:

```text
separate ecclesiastical trust
```

until a particular asset has a reason to be segregated.

Triggers for revisiting the trust:
- church building purchase/donation;
- restricted endowment;
- property donated by Nigeria;
- donor demands permanent purpose restriction;
- explicit denominational trust/reversion agreement;
- material intellectual-property/endowment stewardship need.

This avoids creating a second fiduciary body before it has a distinct job.

## Q6 — What language makes the Nigeria↔Minnesota property / affiliation outcome unambiguous?

**Status: CLAUSE-SET ANSWERED; final drafting remains counsel work.**

Minnesota formally uses the neutral-principles approach for church property/membership disputes. `Piletich v. Deretich` identifies deeds, local charter, state statutes, general-church constitution, explicit trust provisions, and general property law as relevant neutral instruments. `Jones v. Wolf` likewise states that religious societies can use express trust/reversionary clauses to determine property outcomes before a dispute.

Therefore the architecture should make each of these propositions secularly legible.

### Minimum clause set

1. **Entity identity**
   - exact legal name of Minnesota body;
   - exact legal/ecclesiastical identity of Nigerian body;
   - defined terms for “Minnesota Church” and “Global Ministry.”

2. **Affiliation**
   - Minnesota is/is not a constituent branch;
   - what affiliation means;
   - what affiliation expressly does **not** transfer.

3. **Ecclesiastical authority**
   - doctrine;
   - ministerial recognition;
   - branch standing;
   - discipline;
   - final ecclesiastical tribunal/body, if any.

4. **Trustee appointment/removal**
   - exact appointing body/officer;
   - term;
   - removal/vacancy process;
   - required documentary act;
   - effective time.

5. **Temporal authority**
   - Minnesota trustees control local bank, contracts, payroll, local liabilities, records, and ordinary property subject to stated reserved powers.

6. **Property classification**
   Define at least:
   - locally acquired unrestricted property;
   - donor-restricted property;
   - property transferred by Nigeria;
   - property expressly held in trust for Nigeria / denomination;
   - intellectual property / name / marks, if relevant.

7. **No implied property interest**
   Choose one:
   - affiliation alone creates **no** beneficial/reversionary interest; or
   - affiliation creates a specifically described express trust/reversion.
   
   Do not leave this implied.

8. **Disaffiliation / schism trigger**
   - who may initiate;
   - what ecclesiastical act counts;
   - effective date;
   - property result by class;
   - records/name/IP result.

9. **Real-property instrument matching**
   If a building/land is intended to be parent-restricted, make the deed/trust instrument and corporate documents say the same thing. A denominational aspiration that never reaches a legally cognizable property instrument is materially weaker under neutral principles.

10. **Dissolution**
    - disposition solely to qualifying exempt/religious purposes consistent with §501(c)(3);
    - identify whether Nigeria can receive assets only if lawfully eligible or whether a domestic successor is required.

11. **Cross-border funds**
    - Minnesota retains discretion/control over charitable funds;
    - no donor earmark creates an automatic debt to the Nigerian body;
    - grants require Minnesota approval, stated purpose, restrictions, and reporting.

12. **Document hierarchy**
    State which instrument governs:
    - property/title;
    - corporate procedure;
    - ecclesiastical doctrine;
    - conflict between covenant, certificate, bylaws, deed, trust, and denominational constitution.

13. **Amendment authority**
    Define who must concur before a property/affiliation clause changes, while respecting Chapter 315's statutory amendment/member procedures.

### Core drafting law

```text
IF THE PARTIES CARE WHO GETS THE PROPERTY AFTER A BREAK,
WRITE THAT RESULT INTO A LEGALLY COGNIZABLE PROPERTY / CORPORATE INSTRUMENT
BEFORE THE BREAK.
```

## Federal tax overlay

### §508(c)(1)(A)

26 U.S.C. §508(c)(1)(A) provides that the §508 notice/private-foundation presumption provisions do not apply to churches, their integrated auxiliaries, and conventions/associations of churches.

This is not a new exempt category. The IRS states that churches meeting §501(c)(3) are automatically considered tax exempt and may receive deductible charitable contributions without an IRS determination letter.

### “Church” remains factual

The IRS evaluates church status through facts and circumstances, including:
- distinct legal existence;
- creed/form of worship;
- ecclesiastical government;
- doctrine/discipline;
- religious history;
- ministers;
- places of worship;
- regular congregation;
- regular services;
- other facts.

IRS instructions say a congregation or other religious membership group meeting regularly for religious worship is generally required.

Therefore:

```text
RECORDED CHAPTER 315 CERTIFICATE
!=
AUTOMATIC FACTUAL PROOF OF IRS “CHURCH” STATUS
```

The public Impact Makers website does show recurring worship/services and ministry structure in Nigeria, but the Minnesota body itself will need an actual local religious life if relying on church-specific federal treatment.

### Annual return

26 U.S.C. §6033(a)(3)(A)(i) excepts churches, integrated auxiliaries, and conventions/associations of churches from the ordinary annual exempt-organization return requirement.

Minnesota §309.515(b) separately exempts a religious society/organization from its charitable registration/reporting provisions when it is exempt from the federal annual-return requirement under the cited §6033 church/religious exceptions.

## Cross-border money boundary

IRS Publication 526 states that contributions directly to foreign organizations are generally not deductible, and a contribution to a U.S. qualified organization is not deductible if earmarked to go to a foreign organization.

A U.S. charity may conduct/support a foreign program where it:
- approves the program as furthering its own exempt purposes; and
- retains control over use of the contributed funds.

IRS international-activity examination questions specifically ask about:
- pre-grant inquiry;
- written grant agreements;
- repeated-grant review;
- donor earmarking;
- control/responsibility;
- periodic reports;
- shared board/key personnel.

This should become a Corpus OS governance object rather than an informal transfer practice.

## Contradictions and alternatives

### Reading R1 — §315.01(3) is the best “branch” route

**Support:** explicit denominational/body appointment mechanism; direct fit for hierarchical branch.

**Pressure:** we still lack the actual Nigerian constitution/rule that supplies the statutory predicate. If that document does not exist or does not assign trustee selection, the branch cannot manufacture a historical “usage” merely because §315.01(3) is attractive.

### Reading R2 — §315.21 is safer because everything is local and explicit

**Support:** the formation certificate itself can state membership, officer selection, management, and admission; amendment mechanism is explicit.

**Pressure:** it does not contain §315.01(3)'s express parent-body appointment language. Upstream appointment can likely be designed into officer-selection terms, but that exact relationship has not been tested here.

### Reading R3 — separate trust from day one

**Support:** creates visible asset boundary and potential denominational/restricted-property membrane.

**Pressure:** without segregated assets or a real settlor/trust purpose, it adds fiduciary/documentary complexity before solving an actual problem. Chapter 315 already supports church-owned and trust-held property.

### Reading R4 — Nigeria should own/control all Minnesota assets because this is a branch

**Counterevidence:** affiliation and ecclesiastical hierarchy do not themselves establish property ownership under neutral principles; foreign-donation control also makes unlimited upstream access to U.S. charitable funds a bad factual/legal fit.

### F5 — Disaffiliation property policy
The parties must choose the result before counsel can draft it:
- local property remains local;
- parent trust/reversion;
- category-based split;
- purpose-restricted domestic successor;
- another explicit outcome.

### F6 — Form 1023 strategy
No filing is required if the Minnesota body genuinely qualifies as a church, but counsel/accounting advice should decide whether voluntarily seeking a determination letter is worth the administrative visibility/reliance benefit for banking, grantmaking, donors, or institutional counterparties.

### F7 — Foreign transfers / shared personnel
If Nigeria and Minnesota share trustees/officers/personnel or routinely transfer funds, define conflicts, grant-control procedure, documentation, compensation, and related-party review before money begins moving.
