## Treat these as already established unless new authority contradicts them

1. `§315.01(3)` and `§315.21` are alternative Chapter 315 formation routes, not cumulative layers.
2. `§315.01(3)` expressly contemplates trustee choice by a minister, officers, or a denominational/religious **body** when constitution/rules/usages require it.
3. `§315.21` expressly allows a locally constituted religious body to encode admission, membership, officer selection, vacancies, and management in its certificate.
4. Chapter 315 gives trustees substantial temporal/property powers.
5. `§508(c)(1)(A)` is a filing exception for qualifying churches, not a separate exemption outside `§501(c)(3)`.
6. Affiliation does not itself establish property ownership.
7. Minnesota applies neutral-principles analysis to church property disputes.
8. A separate ecclesiastical trust is optional; it should have a real asset/purpose before it exists.
9. U.S. charitable funds should not function as automatic pass-through earmarks to the Nigerian body.

## Search frontier

### F1 — Foreign appointing body under Minn. Stat. §315.01(3)

**Question:** Has a Minnesota court, Attorney General opinion, county recorder practice, treatise, law-review note, church-law resource, or recorded example addressed a trustee-selecting body located outside Minnesota or outside the United States?

Search terms:

```text
"Minn. Stat. 315.01" foreign church
"315.01 subdivision 3" denomination trustees
"315.01(3)" church body appoint trustees
Minnesota religious corporation foreign denomination trustee
Minnesota Chapter 315 hierarchical church foreign
county recorder 315.01 church trustee certificate
```

**Desired discriminator:** evidence that the appointing body may be foreign, or authority imposing a domestic/local limitation.

**Stop condition:** one directly relevant legal authority or a documented absence after checking Minnesota appellate cases, AG opinions, recorder guidance, and leading church/nonprofit secondary sources.

### F2 — Exact recording package for §315.01(3)

Search:

Check county-recorder forms/manuals and historical annotated-statute notes if available.

**Desired discriminator:** recorder instructions or case/AG authority distinguishing election certificates from appointment certificates.

### F3 — §315.05 minister compensation

**Question:** How has Minnesota interpreted the rule that trustees may not fix the minister's salary, which instead is fixed by the relevant voting members?

This matters because an upstream-appointed trustee structure can coexist awkwardly with a locally voted compensation rule.

Search:

```text
Minnesota 315.05 minister salary trustees
"Minn. Stat. 315.05" salary
Minnesota church trustee minister compensation case
Chapter 315 pastor compensation members
```

**Desired discriminator:** case law, AG opinion, annotations, or modern practitioner guidance explaining who votes and whether denominational rules alter the mechanism.

---

### F4 — Foreign denominational property / trust clauses

**Question:** What drafting has Minnesota courts treated as sufficient or insufficient to create an enforceable denominational trust/reversion in local church property?

Start from:
- `Piletich v. Deretich`, 328 N.W.2d 696 (Minn. 1982)
- `Jones v. Wolf`, 443 U.S. 595 (1979)

Then search later Minnesota cases citing `Piletich` on:
- hierarchical churches;
- express trusts;
- deeds;
- denominational constitutions;
- disaffiliation;
- local vs. general-church ownership.

**Desired discriminator:** examples of clause/instrument combinations that courts actually enforced or refused.

---

### F5 — §508(c)(1)(A) practical consequences

**Question:** What does a newly formed Minnesota church gain or lose by relying on automatic church exemption rather than voluntarily seeking a Form 1023 determination letter?

Do not re-prove the statute. Search practical consequences:
- bank onboarding;
- grant eligibility;
- donor reliance;
- state sales-tax applications;
- property-tax exemptions;
- payroll/accounting;
- institutional counterparties.

Prefer IRS, Minnesota Revenue, Minnesota Attorney General, bank/grantmaker policies, and reputable exempt-organizations practitioners.

**Desired discriminator:** concrete consequences, not ideological claims about “508 churches.”

---

### F6 — U.S. church ↔ Nigerian parent money/control

**Question:** What governance patterns best preserve Minnesota charitable control while maintaining genuine ecclesiastical affiliation with a foreign parent?

Search:
- IRS foreign-activity examination guidance;
- grants to foreign organizations;
- earmarking;
- expenditure responsibility analogues where relevant;
- shared directors/officers;
- related-party transactions;
- foreign church affiliation.

**Desired discriminator:** a minimal control/recordkeeping pattern suitable for a small church, not a foundation-scale compliance bureaucracy.

## Critical missing source bundle

The single highest-value nonpublic/public-source acquisition is the Nigerian body’s actual governance packet:

```text
1. official registration / legal entity document
2. constitution / bylaws / canon / rules
3. branch-establishment authority
4. trustee appointment/removal authority
5. officer succession rules
6. exact legal/ecclesiastical name
```

Without that packet, §315.01(3) remains only a strong fit in principle.
